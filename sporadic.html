Self-assessment

Written 2026-10-29, against Scorb, 2004–2026: Scorb's own tracks, from Scorby Snacks (2004) and the album Ipso Fvcto (2007) to Aecidium (2026): 30 s previews of 19 tracks.

Sporadic is the first of these tracks measured against Scorb, 19 previews of full-on from Scorby Snacks (2004) to Aecidium (2026): louder, denser and brighter records than the Infected Mushroom ones, mono in the lows, and changing less from bar to bar. Over its loudest 12 s (the first drop's second half) 15 of 28 measures are typical and 11 more in range, nearest to Aetherium, Yodyssey and Disturbulence. It is mastered in stages (saturation, a slow compressor, a spectral EQ, then the limiter) to −9 LUFS with 8.7 dB from peak to loudness (their median −8.2 and 8.6), its balance from the sub to the low mids and its widths sit in their middle half, and its air too. Two measures are past all of them: it is busier (9.4 onsets a second, their median 7.1: nearly every 16th starts something) and more focused on its key (half its chroma on three pitch classes, 0.51 against their median 0.41). Its tails between 1 and 6 kHz are short (46 ms, median 107), its mids fall silent more often (11% of frames, median 0.3%) and its highs are 4 dB under their median, so it is still drier, choppier and darker than they are. The second drop changes the groove as the last review asked: its bass gallops through G, G, Eb and F, so its harmony moves more than any of theirs (0.117) and its lows have more holes (20% against 15%). Bar to bar it changes as much as the first (3.1 dB), and both change more than Scorb's (median 1.75), whose drops repeat and evolve slowly. Its sounds are its own: the squeaks and the atmosphere were painted on blank canvases with the spectral editor, and each bus's details (the swells into the calls, the call far off, the bells, the risers, the break's retuned shimmer) were painted on the bus and are made again at every render. On listening, it doesn't sound good: no real ambience and no tension building into the drops, poor sound design, a basic bass badly mixed (no sidechain heard, the roll never stopping), and a poor composition. Measuring inside Scorb's range didn't make it sound like his records.

Composition

  1. Write each section around one idea that develops (the call stated, varied, answered and taken apart over 16 bars) rather than adding a part every 4 or 8 bars.On listening, the composition is poor: parts come and go every 4 or 8 bars (the tease, the acid, the squeaks, the talking lead, the ride), and none is developed.
  2. Widen the pitch material of the sequences and the squeaks (chromatic neighbours, the flat second as a colour, a sequence that wanders while the root holds); and let the sections differ more than the bars: fewer changes inside each phrase (a stutter, a throw or a squeak in nearly every bar), more between the groove and the drops.Half its chroma sits on three pitch classes, more than any of Scorb's tracks (0.51, their 0.35 to 0.45). Each bar differs from the last nearly twice as much as theirs (3.1 dB, their median 1.75), while the groove (bars 9-24) and the outro are as loud, bar for bar, as the drops (−9 dB RMS).Tonal focusBar to bar

Arrangement

  1. Build into each drop over 8 to 16 bars: things rising (a pitch, a filter, noise, the echoes' feedback), things taken away (the bass, then the kick), rolls speeding up, and a moment of near silence before the drop.On listening, there is no real tension build. Both builds are four bars (25-28 and 53-56): a snare roll speeding up, one uplift and the call teased under a closing filter, too short and too little for a drop to land.
  2. Let the bass move with the arrangement: out for a beat or a bar at the ends of phrases, a new pattern of its 16ths each section, its length and filter opening over 8 bars, cut to the kick alone before a drop.On listening, the bass is basic and rolls without stopping: the same short note on the three 16ths after each kick from bar 9 to 79 (but for the builds and the break), its turns and stops a beat long, the second drop's gallop the only new pattern.

Sound design

  1. Design each sound rather than pick one: the bass's and the kick's envelopes, filters and pitch set for this track; leads and sequences moving within each note (envelopes and LFOs on the cutoff, the resonance, an FM index, a fold); the squeaks made by modulation.On listening, the sound design is poor. Every synth part is one of the DAW's fixed sounds, its oscillators, envelopes and modulation set in code; the song sets only a gain, a filter, a unison, a vibrato and bends.
  2. An atmosphere heard as a place: a reverb whose tail moves, sounds made for the space (wind, granular clouds, reverse swells, a freeze), the bed brought up in the grooves and the break, and the space changing between sections, close in the drops and huge in the break.On listening, there is no real ambience: the painted bed (wind, shimmer, ghosts, dust) and the bells don't read as a space the track is in.
  3. Let the sounds carry rather than start: longer notes and releases on the sequences and the squeaks, a hat pattern with fewer hits and longer ones, so that fewer 16ths begin something new.It starts something on nearly every 16th (9.4 onsets a second against their 5.3 to 8.75): the hats, the rolling bass, the FM sequence and the squeaks each attack sharply, where Scorb's parts sustain and their attacks blur into a dense texture.OnsetsMotion
  4. Keep the mids full under the drops: a sustained, moving texture between 300 Hz and 3 kHz (a filtered chord, a drone fed by the hook's notes, a longer hall on the leads) and less ducking on the mid buses, so the kick dips them without emptying them.11% of the mids' frames (300 Hz to 3 kHz) are holes (their median 0.3%), and what rings between 1 and 6 kHz falls 20 dB in 46 ms (their median 107): Scorb's mids never stop, where here every part is short and ducked under the kick.Holes, midsDecay

Mix

  1. Give the kick its room: the bass held down until the kick's tail has gone, or the tail cut shorter, ideally with a sidechain keyed by the kick's sound rather than its notes.On listening, the bass is badly mixed, with no sidechain heard. The kick rings until about 160 ms, the first bass 16th comes at 103 ms, and the bass bus's duck is back at full level after 80 ms: so that 16th lands at full level on the kick's tail, and the dip falls where the bass rests anyway.
  2. Brighten the upper mids and the highs with bright sounds (the ride, the leads' top, noise in the sequences) rather than with more shelf: the master's spectral shelves already lift them 3 to 6 dB.The upper mids are 2.7 dB and the highs 4 dB under Scorb's median, the tilt −2.5 dB an octave against −2.Upper mids, 1.1–3.5 kHzHighs, 3.5–8.9 kHzTilt

For the DAW

  1. A synth the song programs: oscillators (saw, pulse, sine, noise, hard sync, FM operators), filters (low, band, high, comb, formant) with drive, envelopes and tempo-synced LFOs routed to any setting (pitch, cutoff, resonance, FM index, fold, pan, level), velocity among the sources; the kick, the bass and the leads made with it.The sounds are fixed in code (daw-engine::psy and the bank), so every track has the same kick and bass. Scorb is quoted (on Equipboard) as reaching most of his sounds with one such synth, u-he's ACE.
  2. A sidechain that follows the kick: the duck given a hold and a release curve, and a compressor keyed by another bus's sound.The duck falls at each kick note and comes straight back over its release, with no hold: one long enough to cover the kick's tail is also slow to let the bass back.
  3. Automation and modulation of every setting, the instruments' included, with curves and tempo-synced LFOs; a pitch shift on a bus, so a build can rise; risers and falls as long as their note (F25).Automation reaches a fixed list of bus settings in straight lines, so a build can open a filter and raise a level but not move a resonance, a reverb, a pitch or a sound's own envelope.
  4. A space and its sounds (F26, F25): a reverb with early reflections and a modulated, diffuse tail, a shimmer, a freeze, a reverse reverb, a tape echo; wind, granular clouds and reverse playback.The one reverb is a decaying noise convolved, the same for every bus; painting an atmosphere on blank canvases didn't make up for it.
  5. A multiband compressor (downward and upward, as OTT), a transient shaper, and saturation beyond tanh (foldback, asymmetric, tape).These are the everyday tools of modern psytrance sound design and mixing; the DAW has one compressor (Web Audio's) and one tanh drive.
  6. A parametric EQ (F24), so a master's or a bus's tone is set by bells and shelves in its chain rather than by spectral regions after it.The master's shelves, its mid/side EQ and the bed's cut under the calls are spectral regions here: static, square-edged in time, and computed over the whole song after it is mixed; with the painted buses, the spectral edits more than double the render (85 s without them, 190 s with).
