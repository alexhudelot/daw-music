Mycelium Cathedral is the first of these tracks to sit inside the 14 Classical Mushroom and B.P. Empire previews wherever it matters: over its loudest 12 s (the second drop) 16 of 28 measures are typical and none is outside them on a side that matters, nearest to B.P. Next to Black Spore Fugue it is mastered rather than squashed (−13 LUFS and 12 dB from peak to loudness, against −8.4 and 7.1), its mids are 7.4 dB wider (the side 9.9 dB under the mid; L/R correlation 0.94, from 0.99), its highs 1.5 dB over the references' median rather than 5.8 under it, and its returns put it on the wet side of their median (ambience −12.9 dB, median −16.6). The first drop changes more from bar to bar than their median (3.1 dB against 2.9); the second, which adds layers rather than changes them, less (2.6). It is still narrower in the mids, fuller in the highs (12% of their frames are holes, their median 35%) and lighter in the sub (−9.7 dB, their median −5) than most of them. On listening, its composition is a clear step up from the last track's, but its melody is still old-school goa rather than Infected Mushroom, and its sound design, mix and master are passable but far from professional. Above all it lacks an atmosphere: ambient sounds and effects of its own, under and between the parts. The numbers miss that: it measures wet because its returns spread what the parts play, but nothing in it plays only to make a space.

Composition

  1. Write the melody as Infected Mushroom did around Classical Mushroom and B.P. Empire rather than as goa: riffs of a few notes with rests between them, syncopated rather than running 16ths; the line carried as much by the sound as by the notes, with bends, dives, octave leaps and a filter or a formant moving in each note; the Phrygian dominant's augmented second as a colour rather than the whole scale, with chromatic passing notes and a turn to the major now and then; the motif passed from sound to sound, its rhythm changed each time.On listening, the melody is still old-school goa. The call, the answer and the acid all run on F# Phrygian dominant (F# G A# B C# D E) with its augmented second from G to A#, the answer and the break's harpsichord are unbroken 16th-note arpeggios, and the break walks the Andalusian cadence (Bm, A, G, F#): goa's three habits at once.
  2. Make the second drop change the groove rather than add to it: a new bassline or a half-time bar, the hook reharmonised or in a new key, layers taken away as well as added.Measured on its own, the second drop changes 2.6 dB from bar to bar against the first's 3.1 (their median 2.9): it doubles the call and the answer and adds a gated choir over the same groove.Bar to bar

Sound design

  1. Give it an atmosphere of its own, under and between the parts: a bed that is always there and slowly changes (air and wind from moving filtered noise, a drone, granular dust), and sounds that come and go by themselves (reverse swells into the calls, risers and falls of any length, the hook's echo far off and pitched down, metallic resonances tuned to the key), so that no gap between the parts is ever empty.On listening, ambient sounds and effects are what it lacks most. It measures wetter than the references' median (ambience −12.9 dB, median −16.6) because its returns spread what the parts play; but no sound in it is there to make a space, and the metric can't tell the two apart.Ambience
  2. Build the leads and the bass from layers, each with its own job (a body, a bite, air), with the sound moving within each note (a filter, a formant, a pitch envelope), and saturated together rather than stacked clean.On listening, the sounds are passable but far from professional: each part is one of the DAW's sounds through its bus, where a produced lead is several, processed together.
  3. Leave holes in the top: fewer bright layers at once, the shaker gated or only in some bars, the hats' room shorter.12% of the highs' 5 ms frames are holes, against the references' median 35%.Holes, highs

Mix

  1. Carve the mix with an EQ once the DAW has one (F24): each part given its band (the pads and the choir cut where the calls sit, the leads cut under 200 Hz and where the kick clicks), the returns' mud taken out between 200 and 500 Hz, and air added on top with a shelf rather than with more bright parts.On listening, the mix is passable but not professional. Its only tone controls are lowpasses and highpasses, which can darken or thin a part but not make room for another.
  2. Widen the mids without widening the highs: a stereo texture between 200 Hz and 2 kHz in the drops, darker echoes, mid-range parts panned apart rather than doubled.The mids' side is 9.9 dB under the mid against their median 6.6, and L/R correlation 0.94 against 0.87. Mastering

  1. Master in stages rather than with a limiter alone: the drums and the bass glued by a compressor on a bus of their own, a little saturation on the master before the limiter, and the balance from the sub to the air set with an EQ's shelves.On listening, the master is passable but not professional. Its loudness and its dynamics measure in range (−13 LUFS, 12 dB from peak to loudness), so what is missing is the tone and the glue, which these numbers don't show; the master has only the limiter and the loudness gain, and the drums' bus no compressor.LoudnessPeak to loudness

For the DAW

  1. A parametric EQ for the buses and the master: bells, shelves and notches, each with its frequency, gain and width, automatable (F24).A bus can only be lowpassed or highpassed, so a part can be darkened or thinned but not carved, and air on top can only come from adding bright parts.
  2. Sounds for an atmosphere (F25): wind and air (noise through slowly wandering resonant bandpasses), a granular cloud (grains of any sound scattered in time, pitch and pan), risers and falls that last as long as their note, and any sound played backwards, so that it swells into its start.The DAW's sounds are nearly all notes and hits; its only sweeps are an uplifter two bars long at 145 BPM and a downlifter, and nothing can be reversed.
  3. Effects for a space (F26): a reverb with early reflections and a slowly modulated, diffuse tail; a shimmer (the reverb fed back an octave up), a freeze that holds it, a reverse reverb swelling into a hit, and a tape echo whose time wanders and whose repeats saturate.The reverb is one burst of decaying noise convolved with the bus, as the JS engine made it, with no early reflections and no movement in its tail; and the echo's repeats are copies, only filtered.
  4. A width by band: mid/side with a crossover, so the mids can be widened while the lows stay mono and the highs as they are (F22).The width works on a bus as a whole, so a part widened for its mids is widened as much in its highs.Width, midsWidth, highs
  5. A leaner render: the buses processed a block at a time rather than each over the whole song, and the file ending where the tail falls silent (F23).This 2 min 12 s song takes over a minute and 1.5 GB to render, which slows trying mixes, and ends in 13 s of silence after its final cut, the tail being reckoned from the highest echo feedback.
